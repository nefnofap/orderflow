  <section id="mod01" class="px-6 md:px-16 lg:px-24 py-16 max-w-5xl mx-auto">
    <div class="module-card reveal">
      <div class="module-header" onclick="toggleModule(this)">
        <div>
          <div class="flex items-center gap-3">
            <span class="w-8 h-8 rounded-full bg-accent/15 flex items-center justify-center text-accent text-xs font-bold font-heading">01</span>
            <h4 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-lg">Auction Market Theory</h4>
            <span class="text-muted text-xs">16 min read</span>
          </div>
          <p class="text-muted text-sm mt-1 ml-11">How markets seek fair value through continuous buyer-seller interaction</p>
        </div>
        <i class="fas fa-chevron-down chevron"></i>
      </div>
      <div class="module-body"><div class="px-7 pb-7 space-y-8">
        <div>
          <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">Auction Market Theory (AMT) is a framework for understanding how financial markets operate at their most basic level. It explains how markets seek fair value through the continuous interaction of buyers and sellers.</p>
          <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed mt-3">AMT is not a trading strategy — it is a lens through which market behaviour can be observed and understood.</p>
        </div>

        <div>
          <h5 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-base mb-3">What is Fair Value?</h5>
          <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">Fair value refers to the price area that facilitates the greatest amount of trade between market participants. At fair value, buyer and seller aggression are closely balanced, resulting in price trading within a relatively tight range while volume remains elevated.</p>
          <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed mt-3">Within the Market Profile framework, fair value is defined as the area containing approximately <span class="text-accent font-semibold">70%</span> of a session's total volume. The Point of Control (POC) serves as the mean of this value area — the price level where the highest level of trading activity occurred. Prices that trade above or below this high-volume fair value area are considered 'unfair' prices, reflecting levels of reduced participation.</p>
        </div>

        <div>
          <h5 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-base mb-3">The Auction Cycle</h5>
          <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed mb-4">The auction cycle describes how financial markets continuously transition between balance and imbalance as they seek fair value.</p>
          <div class="table-wrap">
            <table class="data-table">
              <thead><tr><th>Step</th><th>State</th><th>Description</th></tr></thead>
              <tbody>
                <tr><td class="text-accent font-bold">1</td><td class="text-fg font-medium">BALANCE</td><td class="text-muted">Buyers and sellers agree on price. Volume is elevated. Market is range-bound.</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-accent font-bold">2</td><td class="text-fg font-medium">DISRUPTION</td><td class="text-muted">New information or initiative activity creates imbalance. One side dominates.</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-accent font-bold">3</td><td class="text-fg font-medium">DISCOVERY</td><td class="text-muted">Price moves directionally, searching for new participation levels. Volume thins.</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-accent font-bold">4</td><td class="text-fg font-medium">NEW BALANCE</td><td class="text-muted">Auction reaches an extreme. Participation increases. New fair value is established.</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-accent font-bold">5</td><td class="text-fg font-medium">REPEAT</td><td class="text-muted">Cycle begins again as markets respond to new information continuously.</td></tr>
              </tbody>
            </table>
          </div>
        </div>

        <div>
          <h5 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-base mb-3">Balanced vs Imbalanced Auctions</h5>
          <div class="grid md:grid-cols-2 gap-6">
            <div class="p-5 rounded-xl bg-positive/5 border border-positive/15">
              <div class="flex items-center gap-2 mb-3"><i class="fas fa-arrows-left-right text-positive text-xs"></i><h6 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-sm">Balanced Auction → Ranging Market</h6></div>
              <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">A Balanced Auction is a market environment in which price is actively accepted by participants over time. The market is not attempting to move price higher or lower — it focuses on facilitating trade at accepted levels. The defining characteristic is sustained two-sided activity.</p>
              <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed mt-2">Balanced auctions establish reference areas that provide context for future market activity. These areas frequently provide high-quality reference points for generating trade ideas and risk definition.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="p-5 rounded-xl bg-negative/5 border border-negative/15">
              <div class="flex items-center gap-2 mb-3"><i class="fas fa-arrow-trend-up text-negative text-xs"></i><h6 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-sm">Imbalanced Auction → Trending Market</h6></div>
              <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">An Imbalanced Auction describes a market where price is no longer efficiently accepted and one side becomes dominant. The market is focused on moving price to resolve disagreement, not on facilitating trade. Participation becomes uneven as aggressive participants transact at increasingly higher or lower prices.</p>
              <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed mt-2">Imbalanced conditions offer insight into market intent. Sustained directional movement signals that the market is actively searching for a new area of balance.</p>
            </div>
          </div>
        </div>

        <div>
          <h5 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-base mb-3">AMT Probabilities</h5>
          <div class="table-wrap">
            <table class="data-table">
              <thead><tr><th>Scenario</th><th>Probability</th><th>Implication</th></tr></thead>
              <tbody>
                <tr><td class="text-muted">Market in balance (overall)</td><td class="text-accent font-semibold">~70–80%</td><td class="text-muted">Balance is the most common state</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-muted">Price at value extreme → rotates back</td><td class="text-positive font-semibold">High</td><td class="text-muted">Wait for breakout confirmation</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-muted">Balance breaks → continuation or rotation</td><td class="text-fg font-semibold">~50 / 50</td><td class="text-muted">Neutral; observe participation</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-muted">Price accepts back into value → traverses</td><td class="text-positive font-semibold">~80%</td><td class="text-muted">80% Rule — high-probability trade setup</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-muted">Price accepts back into value → ranges</td><td class="text-negative font-semibold">~20%</td><td class="text-muted">Failed auction — low probability</td></tr>
              </tbody>
            </table>
          </div>
        </div>

        <div>
          <h5 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-base mb-3">Visualising AMT</h5>
          <div class="grid md:grid-cols-3 gap-4">
            <div class="p-4 rounded-lg bg-surface border border-border">
              <div class="text-xs font-semibold text-fg mb-2">Eyeballing (Candlesticks)</div>
              <p class="text-muted text-xs leading-relaxed">Visually assessing market behaviour using candlestick charts without applying structured profiles. Useful for developing intuition and initial context, but has significant limitations — without objective reference points, subjective bias can easily distort conclusions.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="p-4 rounded-lg bg-surface border border-accent/20">
              <div class="text-xs font-semibold text-accent mb-2">Volume Profile</div>
              <p class="text-muted text-xs leading-relaxed">Organises traded volume across price levels. High Volume Nodes (HVNs) represent fair value — areas of strong agreement. Low Volume Nodes (LVNs) highlight rejection — areas of rapid, thin price movement. This makes Volume Profile the most objective AMT visualisation tool.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="p-4 rounded-lg bg-surface border border-border">
              <div class="text-xs font-semibold text-fg mb-2">Time Price Opportunity (TPO)</div>
              <p class="text-muted text-xs leading-relaxed">Organises market activity based on time spent at price. High TPO counts reflect acceptance and balance. Low TPO areas indicate rejection and imbalance. TPO complements Volume Profile by adding a time dimension to the auction analysis.</p>
            </div>
          </div>
        </div>

        <div>
          <h5 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-base mb-3">Acceptance vs Rejection of Value</h5>
          <div class="grid md:grid-cols-2 gap-6">
            <div class="p-5 rounded-xl bg-surface border border-border">
              <h6 class="font-heading font-semibold text-positive text-sm mb-2">Acceptance of Value</h6>
              <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">Acceptance occurs when the market recognises a price area as fair and is willing to conduct business there. Both buyers and sellers actively participate, resulting in increased volume and rotational behaviour.</p>
              <p class="text-accent text-xs mt-3 font-medium">The 80% Rule: when price briefly exits a value area but fails to attract sustained participation, it returns into value and has a ~80% probability of traversing to the opposite end.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="p-5 rounded-xl bg-surface border border-border">
              <h6 class="font-heading font-semibold text-negative text-sm mb-2">Rejection of Value — Failed Auction</h6>
              <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">Rejection occurs when the market tests a price area but fails to attract sufficient participation. Unlike acceptance, rejection signals disagreement — the result is swift directional movement away from the tested level. Rejected zones frequently become important decision points in future sessions.</p>
            </div>
          </div>
        </div>

        <div class="quote-block">"Markets are balanced approximately 70-80% of the time. Balance expectations should always be validated with real-time participation and volume behaviour."</div>
      </div></div>

            <h4 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-lg">Volume Profile</h4>

        <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">Volume Profile (VP) is a market analysis tool that visualises where trading activity has occurred across different price levels. It organises traded volume horizontally by price — not over time — revealing where the market has facilitated the most and least amount of trade.</p>

          <h5 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-base mb-3">Volume Profile Placement Methods</h5>
          <div class="grid md:grid-cols-2 gap-6">
            <div class="p-5 rounded-xl bg-surface border border-border">
              <h6 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-sm mb-3">Composite Profiles</h6>
              <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">A Composite Volume Profile aggregates volume data across multiple trading sessions into a single profile. Commonly used periods include 5-day, 7-day, 30-day, 90-day, 180-day, and 365-day profiles. Shorter composites reflect recent structure; longer composites reveal broader, more established value areas.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="p-5 rounded-xl bg-surface border border-border">
              <h6 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-sm mb-3">Session Profiles</h6>
              <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed"><span class="text-accent">Overnight (ON):</span> 18:00 → 09:30 — represents pre-regular-session positioning<br><span class="text-accent">Regular Trading Hours (RTH):</span> 09:30 → 16:00 — highest participation and volume</p>
              <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed mt-2">Prior session profiles (previous day, week, month) are frequently referenced as important structural context. When price revisits these levels, the market is responding to previously established agreement.</p>
            </div>
          </div>
        </div>

        <div>
          <h5 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-base mb-3">Volume Footprint</h5>
          <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">The Volume Footprint visualises traded volume directly within each individual candle. Rather than viewing a candlestick as a blank representation of price movement, footprint charts reveal how volume was distributed at each price level as the auction unfolded. Used across multiple timeframes — higher timeframes for context, lower timeframes for execution precision.</p>
        </div>

        <div>
          <h5 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-base mb-3">Key Principles</h5>
          <ul class="key-list">
            <li>High volume areas = fair value = balanced, rotational behaviour</li>
            <li>Low volume areas = rejection = fast, directional price movement</li>
            <li>Price at HVN extremes = high-quality decision points</li>
            <li>LVNs act as transition zones between areas of value</li>
            <li>Volume Profile brings objective structure to AMT analysis</li>
          </ul>
        </div>
      </div></div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section id="mod03" class="px-6 md:px-16 lg:px-24 py-8 max-w-5xl mx-auto">
    <div class="module-card reveal">
      <div class="module-header" onclick="toggleModule(this)">
        <div>
          <div class="flex items-center gap-3">
            <span class="w-8 h-8 rounded-full bg-accent/15 flex items-center justify-center text-accent text-xs font-bold font-heading">03</span>
            <h4 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-lg">Time Price Opportunity (Market Profile)</h4>
            <span class="text-muted text-xs">12 min read</span>
          </div>
          <p class="text-muted text-sm mt-1 ml-11">How long price is accepted at different levels throughout a session</p>
        </div>
        <i class="fas fa-chevron-down chevron"></i>
      </div>
      <div class="module-body"><div class="px-7 pb-7 space-y-8">
        <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">Time Price Opportunity (TPO), also referred to as Market Profile, visualises how long price is accepted at different levels throughout a trading session. It organises price activity by time spent at each price, revealing where the market found sustained agreement and where it was quickly rejected.</p>
        <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">Originally developed by J. Peter Steidlmayer in collaboration with the Chicago Board of Trade (CBOT) in the 1980s, TPO adds a critical dimension to auction analysis: <span class="text-fg">time</span>. Each 30-minute period is assigned a letter (A, B, C...). Letters stack at each price level traded during that period.</p>

        <div>
          <h5 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-base mb-3">TPO Chart Construction</h5>
          <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">The session is divided into equal 30-minute intervals. Each interval receives a letter, and every price traded in that interval is marked with that letter. The resulting profile shows time-based acceptance at each price level:</p>
          <ul class="key-list mt-3">
            <li>Many letters = sustained acceptance = fair value = balance</li>
            <li>Few letters = brief visit = rejection = imbalance</li>
          </ul>
        </div>

        <div>
          <h5 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-base mb-3">Market Profile Components</h5>
          <div class="table-wrap">
            <table class="data-table">
              <thead><tr><th>Component</th><th>Definition</th><th>Trading Implication</th></tr></thead>
              <tbody>
                <tr><td class="text-fg font-medium">Value Area (VA)</td><td class="text-muted">Range containing 70% of session TPO activity</td><td class="text-muted">Primary zone of accepted prices; rotational area</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-fg font-medium">Point of Control (POC)</td><td class="text-muted">Price level with highest TPO count</td><td class="text-muted">Session mean; price frequently returns here</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-fg font-medium">Value Area High (VAH)</td><td class="text-muted">Upper VA boundary</td><td class="text-muted">Test of acceptance limits; potential reversal zone</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-fg font-medium">Value Area Low (VAL)</td><td class="text-muted">Lower VA boundary</td><td class="text-muted">Test of acceptance limits; potential reversal zone</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-fg font-medium">Initial Balance (IB)</td><td class="text-muted">First hour of trading (letters A + B)</td><td class="text-muted">Earliest buyer/seller negotiation; key range</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-fg font-medium">Single Prints</td><td class="text-muted">Price levels with only one TPO letter</td><td class="text-muted">Rejection; fast moves when revisited — equivalent to LVNs</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-fg font-medium">Excess</td><td class="text-muted">Two+ consecutive single prints at the extreme</td><td class="text-muted">Clean auction completion; strong rejection; mean reversion likely</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-fg font-medium">Poor High / Poor Low</td><td class="text-muted">One TPO at the extreme (no clear rejection)</td><td class="text-muted">Unfinished business; market likely to return and retest</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-fg font-medium">Unfinished Auction (UFA)</td><td class="text-muted">Two+ TPO periods at exact same extreme</td><td class="text-muted">Incomplete auction; price tends to return to 'finish' it</td></tr>
              </tbody>
            </table>
          </div>
        </div>

        <div>
          <h5 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-base mb-3">Session Configurations</h5>
          <div class="table-wrap">
            <table class="data-table">
              <thead><tr><th>Configuration</th><th>Scope</th><th>Use Case</th></tr></thead>
              <tbody>
                <tr><td class="text-fg font-medium">Overnight (OVN)</td><td class="text-muted">Prior close → 09:30</td><td class="text-muted">Pre-session context and early reference levels</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-fg font-medium">Regular Trading Hours (RTH)</td><td class="text-muted">09:30 → 16:00</td><td class="text-muted">Primary intraday analysis; highest participation</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-fg font-medium">Daily (24h)</td><td class="text-muted">Full 24-hour period</td><td class="text-muted">Complete structural view without session separation</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-fg font-medium">Weekly</td><td class="text-muted">Monday open → Friday close</td><td class="text-muted">Swing trading context; higher-level value areas</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-fg font-medium">Monthly / Yearly</td><td class="text-muted">Full month or year aggregate</td><td class="text-muted">Macro structure; institutional reference levels</td></tr>
              </tbody>
            </table>
          </div>
        </div>

        <div>
          <h5 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-base mb-3">Overnight Inventory</h5>
          <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed mb-4">Overnight inventory refers to the positioning of the overnight session relative to the prior day's settlement (16:15). The settle price serves as the reference for measuring overnight directional bias.</p>
          <div class="table-wrap">
            <table class="data-table">
              <thead><tr><th>Inventory State</th><th>Condition</th><th>RTH Open Tendency (~70%)</th></tr></thead>
              <tbody>
                <tr><td class="text-positive font-medium">100% Long</td><td class="text-muted">ON session entirely above settle</td><td class="text-muted">Market trades lower; unwinds overnight longs</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-negative font-medium">100% Short</td><td class="text-muted">ON session entirely below settle</td><td class="text-muted">Market trades higher; unwinds overnight shorts</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-fg font-medium">Slightly Long / Short</td><td class="text-muted">Majority of ON on one side</td><td class="text-muted">Moderate counter-move tendency; weaker signal</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-muted font-medium">Neutral</td><td class="text-muted">ON corrected within itself</td><td class="text-muted">No strong directional bias from inventory</td></tr>
              </tbody>
            </table>
          </div>
          <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed mt-4">Approximately 70% of the time, the RTH open produces a counter-auction relative to overnight inventory. Overnight participants are generally weaker hands — when the regular session opens with greater liquidity, overnight positions are often liquidated quickly.</p>
          <p class="text-accent text-xs mt-2 font-medium">Exception: If inventory is long and price continues higher at the open — genuine strength. Old business was validated, not unwound.</p>
        </div>
      </div></div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section id="mod04" class="px-6 md:px-16 lg:px-24 py-8 max-w-5xl mx-auto">
    <div class="module-card reveal">
      <div class="module-header" onclick="toggleModule(this)">
        <div>
          <div class="flex items-center gap-3">
            <span class="w-8 h-8 rounded-full bg-accent/15 flex items-center justify-center text-accent text-xs font-bold font-heading">04</span>
            <h4 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-lg">VWAP — Volume Weighted Average Price</h4>
            <span class="text-muted text-xs">6 min read</span>
          </div>
          <p class="text-muted text-sm mt-1 ml-11">Average price weighted by volume — the session's true mean</p>
        </div>
        <i class="fas fa-chevron-down chevron"></i>
      </div>
      <div class="module-body"><div class="px-7 pb-7 space-y-8">
        <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">Volume Weighted Average Price (VWAP) calculates the average price of an instrument throughout a session, weighted by the volume traded at each level. Unlike a simple moving average that treats every price equally, VWAP gives more weight to prices where more contracts changed hands.</p>

        <div class="p-4 rounded-lg bg-surface border border-border">
          <p class="text-muted text-sm"><span class="text-accent font-mono text-xs">VWAP = Σ (Typical Price × Volume) / Σ Volume</span> — The calculation resets at the session start and accumulates. Early in the session, a single large trade can shift VWAP significantly. As the session matures, VWAP stabilises.</p>
        </div>

        <div>
          <h5 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-base mb-3">VWAP as a Reference Level</h5>
          <div class="table-wrap">
            <table class="data-table">
              <thead><tr><th>Price vs VWAP</th><th>Interpretation</th></tr></thead>
              <tbody>
                <tr><td class="text-positive font-medium">Price ABOVE VWAP</td><td class="text-muted">Average buyer is in profit; buying has been dominant; bullish intraday bias</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-accent font-medium">Price AT VWAP</td><td class="text-muted">Neither side has an advantage; market is in balance; equilibrium</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-negative font-medium">Price BELOW VWAP</td><td class="text-muted">Average buyer is underwater; selling pressure dominant; bearish intraday bias</td></tr>
              </tbody>
            </table>
          </div>
        </div>

        <div>
          <h5 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-base mb-3">Standard Deviation Bands</h5>
          <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed mb-4">Standard deviation bands measure how far price has deviated from the session average. They expand during volatile conditions and contract during calm ones.</p>
          <div class="table-wrap">
            <table class="data-table">
              <thead><tr><th>Band</th><th>Coverage</th><th>Interpretation</th></tr></thead>
              <tbody>
                <tr><td class="text-accent font-semibold">±1 SD</td><td class="text-muted">~68% of session activity</td><td class="text-muted">Normal range. Balanced sessions: soft boundary for mean reversion. Trending: price can ride along 1SD.</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-accent font-semibold">±2 SD</td><td class="text-muted">~95% of session activity</td><td class="text-muted">Significant extension from fair value. Balanced: exhaustion signal. Trending: may be reached and sustained.</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-accent font-semibold">±3 SD</td><td class="text-muted">~99.7% of session activity</td><td class="text-muted">Rare. Occurs during strong imbalances or discovery phases. Frequently marks turning points and exhaustion.</td></tr>
              </tbody>
            </table>
          </div>
          <p class="text-muted/60 text-xs mt-2">Treat bands as probability zones, not certainties. Context (balanced vs trending) determines interpretation.</p>
        </div>

        <div>
          <h5 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-base mb-3">VWAP in Different Market Conditions</h5>
          <div class="grid md:grid-cols-2 gap-6">
            <div class="p-5 rounded-xl bg-positive/5 border border-positive/15">
              <h6 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-sm mb-2">Balanced Markets</h6>
              <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">In range-bound sessions, VWAP acts as the equilibrium. Price repeatedly returns to VWAP after testing the extremes. The VWAP line itself stays relatively flat. Price far from VWAP in a balanced market is statistically likely to return to the average. The 1SD bands define where 'far' is in objective terms.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="p-5 rounded-xl bg-negative/5 border border-negative/15">
              <h6 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-sm mb-2">Trending Markets</h6>
              <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">In discovery markets, VWAP acts as dynamic support or resistance rather than a mean reversion target. Price grinds along the 2nd or 3rd deviation bands as one side maintains full control. Pullbacks to the 1st deviation band often provide high-probability continuation entries. Do not fade a trend day just because price has reached the 2nd or 3rd deviation.</p>
            </div>
          </div>
        </div>

        <div>
          <h5 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-base mb-3">Anchored VWAP (aVWAP)</h5>
          <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed mb-4">Anchored VWAP uses the same formula but lets the trader choose the starting point — spanning days, weeks, months, or even years. It reveals the average cost basis of participants who entered after a specific event or price level.</p>
          <div class="table-wrap">
            <table class="data-table">
              <thead><tr><th>Common Anchor Point</th><th>Interpretation</th></tr></thead>
              <tbody>
                <tr><td class="text-fg font-medium">Significant swing high / low</td><td class="text-muted">Average participant cost basis after the move</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-fg font-medium">Session opens (daily, weekly, monthly)</td><td class="text-muted">Broader context for average price since open</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-fg font-medium">Earnings / macro events</td><td class="text-muted">Average cost basis of participants reacting to the event</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-fg font-medium">Gap opens / breakouts from consolidation</td><td class="text-muted">Cost basis of breakout participants</td></tr>
              </tbody>
            </table>
          </div>
          <p class="text-accent text-xs mt-3 font-medium">When multiple anchored VWAPs converge at similar prices, the resulting confluence carries significantly more weight than any single level and often attracts meaningful orderflow when tested.</p>
        </div>
      </div></div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <!-- MODULE 05: DOM -->
  <section id="mod05" class="px-6 md:px-16 lg:px-24 py-8 max-w-5xl mx-auto">
    <div class="module-card reveal">
      <div class="module-header" onclick="toggleModule(this)">
        <div>
          <div class="flex items-center gap-3">
            <span class="w-8 h-8 rounded-full bg-accent/15 flex items-center justify-center text-accent text-xs font-bold font-heading">05</span>
            <h4 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-lg">Depth of Market (DOM)</h4>
            <span class="text-muted text-xs">9 min read</span>
          </div>
          <p class="text-muted text-sm mt-1 ml-11">Real-time display of resting limit orders — the price ladder</p>
        </div>
        <i class="fas fa-chevron-down chevron"></i>
      </div>
      <div class="module-body"><div class="px-7 pb-7 space-y-8">
        <div class="quote-block">"The DOM is a highly personalized and experience-based tool. Reading the DOM cannot be learned solely through written content. This section covers the fundamental concepts and mechanics, but developing real skill requires extensive screen time, repetition, and live market observation."</div>

        <div>
          <h5 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-base mb-3">What is the DOM?</h5>
          <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">The Depth of Market (DOM), also called the Price Ladder, is a real-time display of all resting limit orders at each price level for a given instrument. Unlike Volume Profile or TPO which analyse historical data, the DOM operates entirely in real time — showing where liquidity is sitting now, how fast it is being consumed, and where aggressive participants are willing to transact.</p>
        </div>

        <div>
          <h5 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-base mb-3">DOM Structure</h5>
          <div class="table-wrap">
            <table class="data-table">
              <thead><tr><th>Column</th><th>Type</th><th>Description</th></tr></thead>
              <tbody>
                <tr><td class="text-fg font-medium">Volume</td><td class="text-muted">Historical</td><td class="text-muted">Total contracts traded at each price during the session</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-fg font-medium">Price</td><td class="text-muted">Reference</td><td class="text-muted">The tick ladder — one row per tick increment</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-positive font-medium">Bid</td><td class="text-muted">Passive</td><td class="text-muted">Resting buy limit orders waiting to be filled (provide liquidity)</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-negative font-medium">@Bid</td><td class="text-muted">Aggressive</td><td class="text-muted">Market sell orders hitting bids — aggressive selling (consume liquidity)</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-positive font-medium">@Ask</td><td class="text-muted">Aggressive</td><td class="text-muted">Market buy orders lifting asks — aggressive buying (consume liquidity)</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-negative font-medium">Ask</td><td class="text-muted">Passive</td><td class="text-muted">Resting sell limit orders waiting to be filled (provide liquidity)</td></tr>
                <tr><td class="text-accent font-medium">Bid/Ask Delta</td><td class="text-muted">Repositioning</td><td class="text-muted">Shows whether passive participants are pulling or stacking orders at each level</td></tr>
              </tbody>
            </table>
          </div>
          <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed mt-4">The best bid and best ask sit at the centre of the ladder. Everything above shows resting sell orders; everything below shows resting buy orders. Price moves when aggressive orders consume all resting liquidity at a level.</p>
        </div>

        <div>
          <h5 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-base mb-3">Key DOM Concepts</h5>
          <div class="space-y-4">
            <div class="p-5 rounded-xl bg-surface border border-border">
              <h6 class="font-heading font-semibold text-accent text-sm mb-2">Absorption</h6>
              <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">Absorption occurs when a large resting order defends a price level by absorbing incoming aggressive flow without giving way. You see it when @Bid shows heavy market selling at a level, but the Bid number stays firm or replenishes. Institutions often defend a zone rather than a single price, spreading limit orders across 2–4 ticks. Absorption is most significant when combined with structural context (value area boundary, prior POC, VWAP).</p>
              <p class="text-negative/70 text-xs mt-2">Trapped sellers alone are not absorption. You need to see a response from the buying side: initiation activity, size stepping in, pulling and stacking.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="p-5 rounded-xl bg-surface border border-border">
              <h6 class="font-heading font-semibold text-accent text-sm mb-2">Exhaustion</h6>
              <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">Exhaustion shows that the aggressive side has run out of participants. The pattern: @Bid (aggressive selling) collapses to single digits and zeros at a low while @Ask (aggressive buying) maintains healthy levels. Occurs after a sustained directional move, confirmed by the other side stepping in.</p>
              <p class="text-muted/50 text-xs mt-2">During choppy rotational conditions, thin flow is noise, not a signal. The pattern must persist for more than a few seconds.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="p-5 rounded-xl bg-surface border border-border">
              <h6 class="font-heading font-semibold text-accent text-sm mb-2">Pulling and Stacking</h6>
              <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">Visible through the Bid/Ask Delta column. Pulling: resting orders are removed (reduced confidence). Stacking: orders are added (building genuine support or resistance). When asks are pulling ahead of price while bids are stacking behind, the market is clearing a path for price to move higher. Most significant when combined with absorption or exhaustion.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="p-5 rounded-xl bg-surface border border-border">
              <h6 class="font-heading font-semibold text-accent text-sm mb-2">Spoofing</h6>
              <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">Spoofing involves placing large fake orders with the intention of cancelling them before execution. The tell: large orders appear suddenly and disappear when price approaches without ever getting partially filled. Genuine large orders almost always show partial fills in the @Bid or @Ask columns.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="p-5 rounded-xl bg-surface border border-border">
              <h6 class="font-heading font-semibold text-accent text-sm mb-2">Iceberg Orders</h6>
              <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">Iceberg orders are large institutional orders broken into smaller visible portions. Only a small tip is displayed while the full order is much larger and automatically replenishes. The tell: Volume column shows heavy trading at a level, @Bid or @Ask shows significant flow, but the resting number keeps refreshing to a similar size after each fill.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="p-5 rounded-xl bg-surface border border-border">
              <h6 class="font-heading font-semibold text-accent text-sm mb-2">Finished Auctions</h6>
              <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">A finished auction means the current directional move is completed. Example: @Bid at the low with no @Ask opposite — no aggressive buying at the extreme means no one is willing to buy there anymore. The downside auction is finished. On their own, finished auctions are weak signals — use them only as additional confluence within a complete auction story.</p>
            </div>
          </div>
        </div>

        <div class="p-5 rounded-xl bg-accent/5 border border-accent/15">
          <h6 class="font-heading font-semibold text-accent text-sm mb-2">Context + DOM = Complete Picture</h6>
          <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">The DOM provides real-time confirmation of the market state identified through structural tools. Volume Profile and TPO identify the level. The DOM reveals how participants are actually interacting with it.</p>
          <ul class="key-list mt-3">
            <li>Absorption at support + aggressive selling diminishing → level likely to hold</li>
            <li>Resting bids being pulled + aggressive selling accelerating → structural level may fail</li>
          </ul>
          <p class="text-muted text-sm mt-3">There is no shortcut. Learn the DOM by watching it live, every day, for months. Record sessions, replay, and study. Specialise in one instrument.</p>
        </div>

  <!-- MODULE 08: Trading Psychology -->
  <section id="mod08" class="px-6 md:px-16 lg:px-24 py-8 max-w-5xl mx-auto">
    <div class="module-card reveal">
      <div class="module-header" onclick="toggleModule(this)">
        <div>
          <div class="flex items-center gap-3">
            <span class="w-8 h-8 rounded-full bg-accent/15 flex items-center justify-center text-accent text-xs font-bold font-heading">08</span>
            <h4 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-lg">Trading Psychology</h4>
            <span class="text-muted text-xs">9 min read</span>
          </div>
          <p class="text-muted text-sm mt-1 ml-11">The conscious mind plans. The subconscious mind executes. Training the gap.</p>
        </div>
        <i class="fas fa-chevron-down chevron"></i>
      </div>
      <div class="module-body"><div class="px-7 pb-7 space-y-8">
        <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">Every trader operates with two systems. The <span class="text-fg">conscious mind</span> is the planner — slow, logical, rational. It works when the market is closed, analysing data, defining levels, calculating risk. The <span class="text-fg">subconscious mind</span> is the executor — fast, emotional, protective. It operates when money is on the line and does not care about your plan.</p>
        <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">The gap between knowing what to do and actually doing it is the central problem of trading psychology. The goal is to train the subconscious to trust the conscious plan. This takes repetition, journaling, and discipline over months and years.</p>

        <div>
          <h5 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-base mb-3">Core Psychological Concepts</h5>
          <div class="space-y-4">
            <div class="p-5 rounded-xl bg-surface border border-border">
              <div class="flex items-center gap-2 mb-2"><i class="fas fa-bullseye text-accent text-xs"></i><h6 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-sm">Process Over Outcome</h6></div>
              <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">A winning trade executed outside of your system is a bad trade. A losing trade executed perfectly within your system is a good trade. Outcomes are random in the short term. Process is what produces results over hundreds of trades.</p>
              <div class="mt-3 p-3 rounded bg-positive/5 border border-positive/10 text-sm text-muted"><i class="fas fa-wrench text-positive text-xs mr-2"></i><strong class="text-fg">Fix:</strong> Track execution quality separately from P&L. Score yourself on rule-following, not on money made or lost.</div>
            </div>
            <div class="p-5 rounded-xl bg-surface border border-negative/20">
              <div class="flex items-center gap-2 mb-2"><i class="fas fa-fire text-negative text-xs"></i><h6 class="font-heading font-semibold text-negative text-sm">Revenge Trading</h6></div>
              <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">Revenge trading is entering a trade immediately after a loss to make the money back. It is the single most destructive behaviour in trading. The subconscious wants to eliminate the pain of a loss as fast as possible — so it forces a trade, sizes up, and skips process.</p>
              <div class="mt-3 p-3 rounded bg-positive/5 border border-positive/10 text-sm text-muted"><i class="fas fa-wrench text-positive text-xs mr-2"></i><strong class="text-fg">Fix:</strong> Set a rule: after two consecutive losses, you are done for the session. No exceptions. Two losses don't ruin a week. Five revenge trades do.</div>
            </div>
            <div class="p-5 rounded-xl bg-surface border border-border">
              <div class="flex items-center gap-2 mb-2"><i class="fas fa-bolt text-accent text-xs"></i><h6 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-sm">Fear of Missing Out (FOMO)</h6></div>
              <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">FOMO makes you chase trades that have already left without you. You enter late, with a worse entry, a wider stop, and no edge. The risk/reward is destroyed and the only reason you're in is because you couldn't handle watching it go without you.</p>
              <div class="mt-3 p-3 rounded bg-positive/5 border border-positive/10 text-sm text-muted"><i class="fas fa-wrench text-positive text-xs mr-2"></i><strong class="text-fg">Fix:</strong> When you feel the urge to chase, recognise it as emotion, not analysis. Mark the trade as missed, note what to do differently, and wait for the next setup. The next setup is always coming.</div>
            </div>
            <div class="p-5 rounded-xl bg-surface border border-border">
              <div class="flex items-center gap-2 mb-2"><i class="fas fa-repeat text-accent text-xs"></i><h6 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-sm">Overtrading</h6></div>
              <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">Overtrading is taking trades that don't meet your criteria. Every trade outside your system dilutes your edge. If your system produces 3 high-quality setups per day and you take 12 trades, 9 are noise.</p>
              <div class="mt-3 p-3 rounded bg-positive/5 border border-positive/10 text-sm text-muted"><i class="fas fa-wrench text-positive text-xs mr-2"></i><strong class="text-fg">Fix:</strong> Set a maximum trade count per day. If your system produces 2–4 setups, cap at 4. After 4 trades, you're done. This forces selectivity.</div>
            </div>
            <div class="p-5 rounded-xl bg-surface border border-negative/20">
              <div class="flex items-center gap-2 mb-2"><i class="fas fa-skull text-negative text-xs"></i><h6 class="font-heading font-semibold text-negative text-sm">Tilt</h6></div>
              <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">Tilt is a state where emotional arousal is so high that rational decision-making shuts down completely. You're no longer trading — you're gambling. Triggered by large losses, missed winners, or even large wins creating overconfidence.</p>
              <div class="mt-3 p-3 rounded bg-positive/5 border border-positive/10 text-sm text-muted"><i class="fas fa-wrench text-positive text-xs mr-2"></i><strong class="text-fg">Fix:</strong> Build a tilt checklist. Physical: elevated heart rate, shoulder tension, shallow breathing. Behavioural: clicking faster, ignoring stops, checking P&L every few seconds. Two or more signs = stop trading immediately.</div>
            </div>
          </div>
        </div>

        <div class="p-5 rounded-xl bg-accent/5 border border-accent/15">
          <h6 class="font-heading font-semibold text-accent text-sm mb-3">The Journaling Habit</h6>
          <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed mb-3">Journaling is not optional. It is the mechanism through which the conscious mind teaches the subconscious mind. Every session should be reviewed — not just trades you took, but trades you did not take.</p>
          <ul class="key-list">
            <li>What setups appeared? Did you recognise them in real time?</li>
            <li>If you recognised them, did you act? If not, why not?</li>
            <li>What was your emotional state during each decision?</li>
            <li>Review your journal weekly and look for recurring patterns in losses</li>
          </ul>
          <p class="text-accent text-xs mt-3 font-medium">The patterns in your emotional states will reveal more about your trading problems than any technical analysis ever will.</p>
        </div>

        <div>
          <h5 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-base mb-3">Discipline is a Skill</h5>
          <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed">Discipline is trained through repetition, just like reading the DOM or identifying value areas. Every time you follow your rules despite wanting to break them, you strengthen the neural pathway for discipline. Start small: pick one rule and follow it perfectly for 20 consecutive sessions, then add a second rule.</p>
        </div>

        <div>
          <h5 class="font-heading font-semibold text-fg text-base mb-3">Expectations and Patience</h5>
          <p class="text-muted text-sm leading-relaxed mb-3">Most traders fail because their expectations do not match reality. The learning curve in trading is measured in years, not months. Profitable traders lose on 40–60% of their trades. Equity curves have drawdowns that can last weeks.</p>
          <ul class="key-list">
            <li>Do not set P&L targets — they create pressure to force trades</li>
            <li>Set process targets: follow my rules on every trade, journal every session, review data every Sunday</li>
            <li>Accept losing days, weeks, and months even when doing everything right — variance is real</li>
          </ul>
        </div>

        <div class="quote-block">"The traders who survive long enough to become profitable are not the ones with the best systems. They are the ones who developed the discipline to follow their system when it mattered."</div>
